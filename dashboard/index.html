⚠️ Individualized Behaviour Support Plan (IBSP)

Proactive intervention protocol for interoception dysregulation

EPSB Clinical Mandate
Key Clinical Understanding: When dysregulated, Noah will hit, scratch, bite, and headbutt anyone close to him. Noah does not do this from a place of anger towards any one person, but rather appears to be immensely frustrated that he is not able to communicate with others what he needs.
Primary Antecedents (Interoception Issues): Hunger, thirst, feeling sick, feeling tired, feeling pain, feeling hot/cold, needing bathroom, lights being too bright, loud classroom noises, or food running out.
Level 1: Low-Level Warning Signs
Signs: Furrowed brow and groaning or quietly wailing. (Transitions to Level 2 quickly).
Actions to Diffuse: Immediately try to figure out what Noah needs. Offer bathroom, water, or food. Minimize bright lights and loud noises in the classroom as quickly as possible.
Level 2: Behaviours Requiring More Support
Signs: Biting own hand, hitting self on head, jumping up and down, and screaming.
Actions to Diffuse: Redirect Noah to area by teacher desk. Encourage sensory space to release energy. Have staff bring over visual supports (diapers, water).
🚨 Crisis Management & Safety Plan
When in the teacher desk area, staff should put on arm guards to keep themselves safe. Staff use calm verbal reassurance: "Noah is safe and staff are here to help." Continue offering primary triggers (food, water, bathroom). If dysregulation lasts longer than 10 minutes, clear the room. If longer than 30 minutes, call Noah's mom to come to school to help him regulate.
